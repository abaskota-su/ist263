Background

Dylan Harper was born on March 2, 2006, and grew up in New Jersey.

Harper played at Don Bosco Prep before staying in New Jersey and attending Rutgers University.

Basketball also runs in Harper's family. His father, Ron Harper, played 15 seasons in the NBA and won five championships.

Harper played one year at Rutgers and averaged 19.2 points, 4.5 rebounds, and 3.9 assists per game.

Road to the NBA

The San Antonio Spurs selected Dylan Harper with the second overall pick in the 2025 NBA Draft.

Harper did not need to immediately become the main scorer for San Antonio.

He was able to play more of a supporting role while still getting opportunities to handle the ball, score, and create for teammates.

Harper made the NBA All-Rookie First Team.

Playing Style

Big Downhill Playmaking Guard

I would characterize Harper as a big downhill playmaking guard.

His game is mainly based around getting into the paint and using his size and strength against smaller guards.

Once Harper gets into the paint, he can either finish the play himself or pass to an open teammate.

Offensive Skills

Finishing

Finishing is probably Harper's best offensive skill right now. His size and strength help him finish through contact.

Ball Handling

Harper has a good handle for a player his size. He uses changes of direction and speed to get defenders off balance.

Passing

Harper averaged 3.9 assists per game as a rookie. A lot of his passing comes from getting into the paint and forcing defenders to help.

Shooting

Shooting is one of the biggest areas where Harper can improve.

He shot 34.3% from three-point range during his rookie season.

Defensive Skills

Harper has good size for a guard, which gives him a lot of defensive potential.

He averaged 0.8 steals per game and has enough size to defend different types of guards.

I think he still needs to become more consistent, but he has the tools to become a good defender.

Strengths

  • Attacking the basket
  • Size for a guard
  • Finishing
  • Passing
  • Pace and control

Weaknesses

  • Three-point shooting
  • Mid-range game
  • Free-throw shooting
  • Defensive consistency

Player Archetype

Big Downhill Playmaking Guard

I think this archetype fits Harper because he is a big guard who wants to attack the basket but can also create opportunities for his teammates.

Overall Analysis

Overall, I like Harper's game because he already knows what he is good at.

He uses his size, attacks the basket, and can create opportunities for teammates.

The biggest thing I would like to see him improve is his jump shot. If teams have to respect his three-point shot more, his driving game could become even better.
