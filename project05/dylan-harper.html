Dylan Harper

Height: 6'5" | Weight: 215 lbs | College: Rutgers | Drafted: 2nd Overall, 2025

Background

Dylan Harper was born on March 2, 2006, and grew up in New Jersey. Harper attended Don Bosco Preparatory High School, where he became one of the highest-ranked players in the country and one of the top recruits in the 2024 class.

Harper decided to stay in New Jersey and play at Rutgers University. This was a big deal for Rutgers because Harper was one of the highest-ranked recruits of all time within the program. He decided to play alongside another valuable recruit Ace Bailey.

Basketball also runs in Harper's family. His father, Ron Harper, played 15 seasons in the NBA and won five championships. His older brother, Ron Harper Jr., also played at Rutgers before reaching the NBA.

Harper only played one season at Rutgers during 2024–25. As a freshman, he averaged 19.2 points, 4.5 rebounds, and 3.9 assists per game while shooting 48.7% from the field.

Harper also set the Rutgers freshman scoring record and earned Big Ten All-Freshman honors.

Playing Style

Big Downhill Playmaking Guard

I would characterize Dylan Harper as a big downhill playmaking guard.

A downhill guard is basically someone who is at his best when attacking the basket especially in transition. Harper does not depend on taking a lot of difficult jump shots. Instead, he uses his size, strength, ball handling, and change of pace to get into the paint. Alongside his high basketball IQ to slow the game down as a whole.

Harper's size is one of the first things that stands out to me. At 6-foot-5 and around 215 pounds, he is much bigger than a lot of point guards.

This gives him the ability to use his body to protect the ball and absorb contact when he attacks the rim, making it harder for defenses to guard him.

Harper is also left-handed, which can sometimes make him harder to defend because the ability to drive left, where man to man defenses aren't prepared for.

Another thing I like about Harper's game is that he does not always play at one speed. He can slow down, wait for a defender to react, and then suddenly attack. This allows him to use his change of pace in different ways inside and outside the paint.

Offensive Skills

Finishing

One of Harper's best offensive skills is his finishing around the basket.

During his rookie season, about 40.5% of his field-goal attempts came from within three feet of the rim. He made about 65.8% of those shots.

That shows how important attacking the rim is to his game.

Harper uses his size and strength well when he drives. Smaller guards can have a hard time stopping him once he gets past them because he can absorb contact and still finish the play.

Ball Handling

Harper has very good ball-handling ability for someone his size.

He does not always rely on flashy moves. Instead, he uses hesitations, changes of direction, and changes of speed to get defenders off balance.

His handle also allows him to play as either the main ball handler or next to another guard. I think this fits San Antonio well because they do not have to force Harper into only one role.

Passing

Harper is also a good playmaker.

He averaged 3.9 assists per game during his rookie season despite only playing around 23 minutes per game.

A lot of Harper's passing opportunities come from his ability to get into the paint. When another defender comes over to help stop his drive, somebody else is usually left open.

Harper can then either finish at the basket or pass the ball to that teammate.

I would not describe Harper as a pass-first point guard, but I think his passing is good enough that teams cannot only worry about him trying to score.

Shooting

Shooting is still one of the areas where Harper can improve.

He shot 34.3% from three-point range during his rookie season. Only about 27% of his field-goal attempts were three-pointers, which shows that Harper would rather get toward the basket.

If Harper becomes a more consistent outside shooter, defenders will have to play closer to him. That would make it even harder to stop his drives because they would not be able to give him as much space.

Road to the NBA

The San Antonio Spurs selected Dylan Harper with the second overall pick in the 2025 NBA Draft, directly after Cooper Flagg was taken first overall by Dallas.

Harper entered the league as a 6-foot-5 guard who already had great size and strength for his position. Unlike Flagg, Harper was not immediately expected to become the main scorer for his team.

San Antonio already had a lot of young talent, which allowed Harper to play more of a supporting role while still getting opportunities to handle the ball and create offense.

His size also gave the Spurs the ability to use him at either point guard or shooting guard depending on who else was on the court.

  • 3.4 rebounds per game

Harper ended up making the 2025–26 NBA All-Rookie First Team.

I think his rookie season was pretty impressive considering he only played around 23 minutes per game and was not expected to be San Antonio's main scorer. He still showed that he could score, pass, and contribute without needing the ball in every possession.

Defensive Skills

Harper has the physical tools to become a very good defender.

His height and strength give him an advantage compared to smaller guards. He can defend guards but also has a better chance of dealing with larger players if he gets switched onto them.

Harper averaged 3.4 rebounds and 0.8 steals per game during his rookie season.

His size also helps him contest passes and rebound better than some smaller guards.

I think defense is still an area where Harper can improve, especially when it comes to being consistent every possession. However, he already has the size and athleticism needed to become a good defender.

Strengths

Attacking the Basket

Harper's biggest offensive strength is his ability to get into the paint. A lot of his offense starts with him attacking the basket and forcing the defense to react.

Size for a Guard

At 6-foot-5 and 215 pounds, Harper is much bigger than many traditional point guards. His size helps him protect the ball, finish through contact, rebound, and defend different players.

Finishing

Harper is a very good finisher around the basket. He shot about 65.8% on attempts within three feet of the rim during his rookie season.

Passing

Harper is not only looking to score when he gets into the paint. He can also find open teammates and averaged 3.9 assists per game as a rookie.

Pace and Control

One thing I like about Harper is how he changes speeds. He does not always try to play as fast as possible, which makes it harder for defenders to know when he is going to attack.

Weaknesses

Three-Point Shooting

One of Harper's main areas for improvement is his three-point shooting.

He shot 34.3% from three during his rookie season. This is respectable for a rookie, but teams are still much more worried about Harper driving than shooting.

If he becomes a better three-point shooter, he would become much harder to guard because defenders would not be able to give him as much space.

Mid-Range Game

Harper does most of his scoring near the basket and does not take many mid-range shots.

Adding a more reliable pull-up jumper could give him another option when defenders stop him from getting all the way to the rim.

Free-Throw Shooting

Harper shot 75.6% from the free-throw line during his rookie season.

Since a lot of his game is based around driving and drawing contact, becoming a better free-throw shooter could help him score more efficiently.

Defensive Consistency

Harper has the size and athleticism to become a good defender, but he is not completely consistent yet as he struggled to defend certain players throughout his rookie year.

I think improving his positioning and awareness will come as he gets more experience playing against NBA guards, often when transitioning from college to NBA basketball, defense is one of the hardest learning curves.

Player Archetype

Big Downhill Playmaking Guard

I would describe Dylan Harper's archetype as a big downhill playmaking guard.

Shot Profile

Using the shot profile chart, most of Dylan Harper's shots are taken within the paint and mid range area.

  • 21.8% of his shots fell within 3 feet of the basket
  • 31.1% of his shots fell between 3–10 feet
  • 15.1% of his shots fell between 10–16 feet
  • 11.6% of his shots fell between 16 feet to the three-point line
  • 20.4% of his shots were three-point attempts

He shot approximately 74.2% of his shots within three feet, so his most efficient area in the paint is near the basket. Meanwhile, his efficiency dropped off a bit as he moved towards the 3 point line.

His low shooting percentage of 29.5% from three-point range highlights his need for improvement in his long-range shooting.
